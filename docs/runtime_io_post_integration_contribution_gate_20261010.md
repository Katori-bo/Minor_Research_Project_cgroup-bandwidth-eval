## Starting point

The current paper's contribution is scoped prospective evidence of forecast
adequacy and calibration-transfer limits. The underlying estimators are
established. Both successful and adverse episodes remain in the manuscript;
friend-owned independent V31 is pending and excluded. Earlier artifacts and
manuscript snapshots remain separate from the integrated revision.

The [completed capability assessment](runtime_io_transfer_consolidation_20261010.md)
already covers periodic-server analysis, profile/load prediction, power-aware
adaptation and state-conditioned uncertainty. Do not restart a broad novelty
search or rename ordinary conditioning, confidence intervals or abstention
as a new method. A further contribution must add an identifiable capability
or establish a specific empirical/causal finding beyond the current record.

## Most relevant unresolved question

**Can an observable available before a future forecasting episode distinguish
likely transfer failure from accurate transfer, at useful coverage and a fair
acquisition cost?** Policy/rate readbacks, calibration agreement and the existing
short-warmup checks have not supplied a validated answer. The new accurate
episode does not reveal which unmeasured condition changed.

Before implementing a guard or collecting new traffic, write down:

1. The exact observable and when it becomes available, plus the physical or
   statistical relationship expected to connect it with future response changes.
   A future measurement error or later counter delta cannot be an advance input.
2. The additional capability relative to ordinary profile/state/uncertainty
   methods, granting each comparator its required inputs and charging the same
   sample, observation and decision-time costs.
3. The prediction target and unit: future-episode probability error, incorrect
   assurances, answer coverage, individual-cohort behavior and acquisition cost.
   Conditional accuracy intervals alone are not assurance-error calibration.
4. A feasible independent episode design with a frozen rule and thresholds,
   retained unsuccessful/domain outcomes and no favorable retries. Existing
   campaigns are known development evidence for a newly selected method.

The present records lack both a demonstrated new observable-to-future relation
and a sufficiently diverse labelled episode history. Until those prerequisites
exist, this is a held question, not a justified new method experiment.

## Other routes have different prerequisites

A causal NGINX extension needs measurements or interventions distinguishing
the proposed mechanism. Client header/body elapsed intervals do not isolate
server CPU, scheduler delays, frequency or internal queues. A generic trace
collection is not itself a contribution; specify what competing explanation
it could rule out before instrumenting the apparatus.

A published-predictor evaluation first needs a compatible scheduler, input and
output contract. Do not compare our miss-probability errors to a paper's quantile
relative errors, remove its required state inputs, or attribute an unsupported
adapter's behavior to the full published method. A source/assumption audit is
the initial gate, not a reason to launch a broad implementation campaign.

The friend's V31 run can strengthen the hardware scope of the existing claim
after complete verification. It cannot explain the NGINX cause by itself.
If its outcomes inform a newly selected reliability rule, preserve that role
as development and acquire separate confirmation evidence; do not count the
same outcomes as independent validation of the new rule.

## Next decision

Use this gate for one bounded, read-only feasibility assessment of a concrete
candidate when there is evidence to specify it. No such candidate is established
by the current integration. If ordinary methods explain or supply the claimed
capability, stop the custom-method route and retain the empirical paper. Do not
invent a method or another experiment merely to label the work novel.
