# Offline SLO-decision audit: declared development contract

11 October 2026. The user selected the question: when does apparently accurate
calibration lead to a wrong SLO decision, and what evaluation exposes it?
Historical outcomes are already known. This is a fixed saved-data development
audit, not fresh confirmation, a deployment assurance, or a new method claim.
No manuscript change, hardware run, settings change or held observer follows.

## Population and target

Retain all 18 local Go V31 and 78 completed NGINX target cohorts: original 18,
power 24, rate 12 and fresh panel 24. These are five histories on one host, with
shared references and six chronological blocks per history. V30 has a different
periodic-model contract and is outside this ordinary response-CDF audit; it
retains its historical result. Friend-owned V31 remains pending and excluded.

The illustrative SLO requires no more than 5% of all scheduled target offers
to fail or exceed finite deadline D. Equality in latency is timely; equality
at the 5% miss budget meets the empirical SLO. Score each complete target window,
not successful-only responses or an outcome-selected subset. Observed window
compliance is distinct from an unobserved population or future-deployment claim.

Retain historic Go 10/11/12 ms endpoints (11 ms was secondary) and the historic
NGINX 20.565395 ms endpoint. Retain all five originally frozen fresh-panel
cutoffs as a separate family. Add the same new calibration-only grid in every
history: 90%, 95%, 97.5%, 99%, 99.5% nearest ranks ceil(q*n)-1, counting failed
calibration offers as infinite. Cutoffs use only each cohort's ordinary selected
calibration reference: Go complete-response 676, original NGINX pooled 1,600,
power/panel policy-matched 800, rate ordinary fixed-5/s 800. Retain unavailable
finite cutoffs when failure mass reaches a chosen rank. Quantile labels are not
independent evidence, and duplicate deadlines remain explicitly labelled cells.

These new deadlines are development questions, not actual user-supplied SLOs.
A 95th-percentile calibration cutoff makes its own estimated miss fraction <=5%
by construction when finite: self-fit is not evidence of accurate future transfer.
No quantile, error tolerance, budget or subgroup is tuned after new scoring.

Point decision: forecast p<=.05 means meets; otherwise exceeds. Also show the

for each empirical reference. Its decision is meets if upper<=.05, exceeds if
lower>.05, otherwise inconclusive. The band requires independent common-population
offers within its pool; pooled warmup/calibration additionally requires a shared
distribution. It estimates that population, not a later changed population.
No transfer guarantee or simultaneous coverage across reference pools is claimed.

## Scores and finite-target uncertainty

Report full confusion counts: meets/observed-meets, meets/observed-exceeds
(false assurance), exceeds/observed-meets (unnecessary rejection), and
exceeds/observed-exceeds. For interval decisions show inconclusive separately,
answer coverage, false assurance per all cells and per meets decision, and
unnecessary rejection per observed-meets cell. Do not hide zero-coverage methods.

Report absolute and signed probability errors, empirical SLO violation magnitude,
distance of forecast to the budget, and decision errors with absolute error<=5 pp.
The latter is a retrospective evaluation label, not a guard or scientific gate.
Report campaign, policy, rate and six-block counts, without request-level pooling
or an overall method-ranking confidence interval. Preserve failed/throttled
cohorts and their observations. Inspect saturation at the historical endpoint
separately; accuracy there does not predict decisions at different deadlines.

For target miss counts compute exact two-sided Clopper-Pearson intervals under
the conditional IID Bernoulli model, both 95% per cell and Bonferroni family 95%
across every declared finite cohort/deadline cell. Target deadlines are fixed
from calibration before target loading; target/calibration independence is still
an assumption. The family correction needs no independence between cells.
Use these only to distinguish boundary-sized empirical flips from decisions
whose target lower bound exceeds 5% under those hypothetical assumptions.
They do not validate offer independence or infer the cause of transfer failure.
Do not fit a decision threshold to these intervals or select a winning subgroup.
